# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Local Scottsdale-area diners researching where to eat or order from tonight —
often on a phone, mid-decision between a handful of open tabs. Some are
first-timers deciding whether Canton Dragon looks like "our kind of place";
others are regulars checking hours, the menu, or grabbing the online-ordering
link before they leave the house. A smaller slice is planning a group visit
or event and wants reassurance the space fits the occasion.

## Product Purpose

A single-page marketing site for Canton Dragon Asian Grill & Bar that replaces
the outdated cantondragonscottsdale.com. It exists to convert browsing into
two concrete actions: an online order placed through the Heartland ordering
system, or a decision to visit in person (informed by hours, address, and
map in the Visit section). Success looks like a visitor leaving with no open
questions about what the place is, what's on the menu, or how to get there —
and clicking one of those two paths before they leave the page.

## Brand Personality

Warm and inviting, with modern, confident drama underneath — an upscale
Asian grill & bar that doesn't feel like a template. The existing charcoal +
gold duotone hero, lacquer-red/gold palette, and Fraunces/Hanken Grotesk
pairing already carry this: refined enough to feel like a step up from
everyday casual dining, but never cold or corporate. Voice should read as
confident host, not sales copy — inviting people in rather than selling at
them.

## Anti-references

Avoid the generic local-restaurant-template look: stock-photo hero carousels,
bolted-on review widgets, cluttered nav bars, default Google Maps embeds,
and menu PDFs. The site should feel like a considered, custom-built
experience — real photography, an intentional palette, and a single
confident narrative — not a WordPress theme with the logo swapped in.

## Design Principles

- **Warmth with drama, not either/or.** The charcoal/gold duotone and dark
  bar-and-lounge mood should still feel welcoming, not austere or cold.
- **Two paths, always visible.** Every major section should keep "order
  online" and "plan a visit" within easy reach — never force a scroll hunt.
- **Real photography over stock.** The restaurant's own food, interior, and
  drink photography carries the brand; no generic stock imagery.
- **Motion earns its place.** Scroll reveals and duotone effects should feel
  intentional and premium, never gratuitous — and must degrade gracefully
  under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **No template tells.** Reject anything that reads as a swapped-logo
  template: custom nav, custom menu tabs, custom map styling, not
  off-the-shelf widgets.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Match and maintain what's already implemented: semantic landmarks,
skip link, labeled icon buttons, visible focus states, focus-trapping modals
(mobile drawer, lightbox) that restore focus on close, `Esc`-to-close, and
full `prefers-reduced-motion` support across all animation.
